A PRACTICAL JOURNALING GUIDE

How to keep a cannabis journal.

Keep a useful record of what you tried and what you noticed. Start with a few repeatable details, then build a diary you can actually search and use.

What is a cannabis journal?

A cannabis journal, sometimes called a strain diary or weed journal, is a personal record of cannabis products and your observations. It can be a notebook, a spreadsheet or an app. The useful part is consistency: saving enough detail to recognize a product later and understand what your rating meant.

Strain Diary is an iPhone app designed for this routine. You can also use the template below in any notes app or on paper.

What to write in each entry

  1. Identify the product. Record the strain, brand and product type. Add a packaging photo and batch identifier when available.
  2. Save the date and label details. Copy the potency and terpene values as written, including units. Leave missing information blank rather than guessing.
  3. Record the context. Note the method, amount if known, and any context you want to remember. Keep observations separate from information copied from the package.
  4. Describe your experience. Use your own words for flavor, aroma and effects. Record observations you did not like as well as those you did.
  5. Choose a consistent rating. Decide what your scale means and use it the same way each time. Add a sentence explaining the score.

You do not need to complete every field every time. A short entry you can understand a month later is more useful than a complicated system you abandon.

Make your notes easier to compare

Keep entries for different products or batches distinguishable, even when they share a strain name. Use the same rating scale, preserve the units printed on labels, and mark unknown values as unknown.

When you review your diary, look for repeated observations and exceptions. A single entry describes that occasion; it does not establish why an experience happened or predict the next one.

Cannabis journal questions

Do I need terpene or laboratory data to start?

No. Start with the product name, date, rating and your own notes. Add label details when available, and leave unknown values blank.

Should every entry be long?

No. A few consistent fields and a sentence about what stood out can be enough. Add detail when it helps you recognize or compare an entry later.

Can a journal tell me which product will work next time?

A journal can help you review your past observations. It cannot guarantee future effects or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
