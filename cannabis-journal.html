A cannabis journal is your personal record of products and observations. Also called a strain diary or weed journal, it can be as simple as a few repeatable fields.

Start with these three things ↓

KEEP IT SIMPLE

Three things
worth writing down.

Record what you know. Leave the rest blank. You can always add more detail later.

01

What was it?

Enough detail to recognize the product later.

  • Strain, brand and product type
  • A photo of the packaging or product
  • Batch, potency and terpenes, if listed
Keep the units. Copy label values as written. Missing information? Leave it blank.
02

What did you notice?

Your observations, in your own words.

  • Date, time and method
  • Amount, if known, and useful context
  • Aroma, flavor and personal effects
Keep it personal. Separate what the label says from what you experienced.
03

What’s the takeaway?

The sentence you’ll be glad you saved.

  • A rating using your usual scale
  • Why you chose that rating
  • What you liked and what you didn’t
Keep it consistent. Use the same scale so your old ratings still mean something.

ONE LAST THING

You don’t have
to get it perfect.

Start small. A product name, date and a sentence are a useful beginning.

Do I need terpene or laboratory data to start?

No. Start with the product name, date, rating and your own notes. Add label details when available, and leave unknown values blank.

Does every entry need to be long?

No. A few consistent fields and a sentence about what stood out can be enough. Add detail when it helps you recognize or compare an entry later.

Can a journal predict what will work next time?

A journal helps you review past observations. It cannot guarantee future effects or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
