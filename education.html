CANNABIS EDUCATION

Understand what you log.

Clear, practical explanations for reading labels, comparing products and recording your own experience—without hype or medical claims.

START WITH THE LABEL

Turn product details into useful context.

Cannabis labels can pack potency, serving size, batch details and lab results into a small space. The goal is not to chase the biggest number. It is to understand what the package says, save the details consistently and compare them with your own notes over time.

“A label describes the product. Your diary describes your experience.”
Use both when comparing strains.
01 · CANNABINOIDS

Read potency in context

THC and CBD values describe measured cannabinoid content, not a guaranteed experience. Compare values only when the units and product types match.

02 · TERPENES

Notice the aroma profile

Terpenes are aromatic compounds such as myrcene, limonene and pinene. Record the full profile instead of treating one terpene as a prediction.

03 · YOUR NOTES

Track what varies

Method, amount, setting and time can change an experience. Consistent notes make comparisons more useful than strain names alone.

THE BASICS

Four ideas worth knowing.

What is a certificate of analysis (COA)?

A COA is a laboratory report tied to a product or batch. It may list tested cannabinoids, terpenes and screening results. Check that the product or batch information matches the package, and remember that formats and required tests vary by jurisdiction.

Do strain names guarantee the same product?

No. Products sold under the same strain name can differ by grower, batch, genetics, storage and testing. Saving the brand, batch, date and label details gives the name more useful context.

Can terpene percentages predict effects?

Not reliably on their own. Terpene data can help describe and compare aroma profiles, but an individual result depends on many factors. Treat profile information as a comparison tool rather than a promise.

What makes a useful strain journal entry?

Record enough to recognize the product later: strain and brand, product type, date, amount or method, label details and a short note about your experience. A consistent rating scale is more useful than a complicated one you stop using.
