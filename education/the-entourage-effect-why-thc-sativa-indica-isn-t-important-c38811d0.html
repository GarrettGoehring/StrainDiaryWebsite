The Entourage Effect: Why THC % & Sativa/Indica isn’t important!

garrett goehring · September 5, 2026

A higher THC percentage doesn’t automatically mean a better or even stronger experience.

Cannabis contains THC, CBD, other cannabinoids, and aromatic compounds called terpenes. The idea behind the entourage effect is that these compounds may interact and influence the overall experience rather than THC acting completely on its own.

Terpenes like limonene, myrcene, pinene, caryophyllene, and linalool contribute to a strain’s aroma and flavor and may also play a role in how the experience feels. Research into exactly how much terpenes alter cannabis effects is still developing, so the entourage effect shouldn’t be treated as completely proven science yet. (MDPI⁠￼)

Sativa, Indica & Hybrid Aren’t Very Reliable

The traditional Sativa, Indica, and Hybrid labels are commonly used to predict effectsSativa for energetic, Indica for relaxing, and Hybrid somewhere in between but real cannabis doesn’t fit neatly into those categories.

Research has found that Sativa and Indica labels do not reliably represent the actual genetics or full chemical profile of a strain. Two products both labeled “Indica” can have very different terpene and cannabinoid profiles and potentially produce very different experiences. (Nature⁠￼)

That’s why looking at the actual chemistry of the flower can be more useful than relying only on the name printed on the jar.

THC % Is Only One Number

THC percentage tells you how much THC is present it doesn’t tell you the entire experience.

A 20% THC strain with a terpene profile that works well for you may feel much more enjoyable than something testing at 30% THC. Dose, tolerance, cannabinoids, terpenes, consumption method, and your own body chemistry can all influence the experience.

How Strain Diary Handles It

Strain Diary goes beyond the traditional Sativa, Indica, and Hybrid labels.

When terpene information is available, Strain Diary uses the strain’s terpene and cannabinoid profile to create a more chemistry-based classification and show potential effects associated with that profile.

Instead of assuming every “Sativa” will feel energetic or every “Indica” will make you tired, the app helps you look at what is actually in the strain and learn which terpene profiles tend to work best for you.

The more strains you log, the easier it becomes to notice patterns between their chemistry and your personal experience.

Bottom line: Don’t choose cannabis based only on THC percentage or whether the package says Sativa, Indica, or Hybrid. Pay attention to the cannabinoids, terpenes, dose, and most importantly how different profiles actually affect you.
