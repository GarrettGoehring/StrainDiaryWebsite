Cannabis Carts: Why Heat Matters

Distillate, live resin, and live rosin aren’t interchangeable. The oil and cartridge hardware affect how they handle heat. Too much heat can damage flavor, overheat the cartridge, and increase exposure to unwanted breakdown products.

Distillate

Distillate is a highly refined cannabis extract, often blended with botanical-derived terpenes (BDT) from other plants or cannabis-derived terpenes (CDT) extracted from cannabis to add flavor and adjust consistency.

Distillate cartridges can often tolerate higher voltage settings than live resin or live rosin cartridges, depending on the oil formulation and hardware. That doesn’t mean added terpenes protect the oil from heat or that distillate can’t burn. Excessive heat, long draws, and back-to-back hits can still damage flavor and overheat the cartridge. Follow the manufacturer’s recommended settings and allow it to cool between uses.

Live Resin

Live resin is made from fresh-frozen cannabis to preserve more of the plant’s original aromatic profile. Excessive heat can dull those flavors. Repeated hits without cooling can also overheat the oil and contribute to leaks or clogs. Give the cartridge time to cool between uses.

Live Rosin

Live rosin is a solventless extract with a delicate flavor profile that deserves careful heat control. Prolonged heating can overwhelm that flavor, and the cartridge needs hardware suited to its oil. Follow the brand’s guidance rather than assuming every rosin cartridge needs the same voltage. A voltage number alone doesn’t tell you the oil’s temperature.

Skip the Blinkers

A “blinker” means holding a draw until the device reaches its automatic cutoff. That cutoff is a limit, not a target. Long draws and back-to-back use can build up heat with any oil type.

Allow the cartridge to cool between uses. A few seconds may not be enough if it still feels warm. If it tastes burnt or unusually harsh, stop using it and check the manufacturer’s guidance. Turning up the heat can make the problem worse.

Treat the manufacturer’s instructions as the starting point. Oil type matters, but the oil and hardware work together.
