Why I built it

I couldn’t find a strain tracker I actually liked.

The ones I tried had clunky layouts, missing strain data and way too much manual entry. None of them made it easy to remember which jar was actually worth buying again.

Honestly, I just wanted to remember the good jars

I wanted one place to save the strain, brand, batch, terpenes, flavor, effects, how I used it and whether I would buy it again—without filling out a huge form every time.

So I decided to build it myself

One night after taking some dabs, I thought I could probably make a better tracker. So I started building one. Strain Diary is the app I wanted to use: it handles as much of the data entry as possible and keeps the details I will actually want later.

I use it too

Strain Diary is independently built and maintained by me. I use the app, read the feedback and make the changes. If anything feels confusing or harder than it should be, I want to fix it.

Where I want to take it

The long-term goal is to put everything I would want from a cannabis app in one place: label and COA scanning, strain data, terpenes, crosses, discovery, education, connected-device controls, social features and chats, while keeping personal information private by default.

It is still growing, and feedback from testers has already changed a lot of it. If you have an idea or notice something that feels wrong, let me know.
